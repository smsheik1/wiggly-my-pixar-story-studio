# script

Criteria: facts, relationship, clarity, emotional-purpose, timing, common-sense.

- facts: Every name, place, object, date and age traces to a locked answer or an approved clarification. The shell proves that a quoted span matches its cited field word for word; you check that the right person is credited and the line is used the way the answer describes it.
- relationship: The recipient and every person the narration names are the people the answers describe, in the right role. The recipient may be a child, a partner or a grandchild.
- clarity: A listener can follow it by ear, in plain words, with no unexplained reference. Judge the language against the recipient named in the packet; never ask for child-level language when the recipient is an adult.
- emotional-purpose: Each beat has a discernible feeling. Stock phrases ("my whole world," "meant everything") are a note in the evidence of a passing check. Fail only a beat that is nothing but stock phrases, with no concrete detail from the answers.
- timing: Estimate only, as the core guide describes.
- common-sense: The proposed cast is the minimum the story needs, and every person the narration names who must be on screen is in it. Actions are physically possible. Nothing in the script needs an invented fact to make sense.

Not defects: a nonchronological story, an ending that isn't an image, no fear or cost named, no quoted line, a plain direct sentence such as "I love you" that earlier specifics have earned, a general line that specifics set up.
