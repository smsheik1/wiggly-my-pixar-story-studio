# shotIntentions

Criteria: coverage, timing, scene-fit, references, staging, continuity.

- coverage: Every scene has a shot and all four beats are covered. Every beat that is about a person shows that person; a script that says "he pulled the tarp" with a shot of the narrator pulling it fails.
- timing: The shell verifies the 15.0-second sums. You judge whether each shot's action fits its length and the visual payoff sits on the narration's key line.
- scene-fit: Shot action and emotion serve the scene and don't contradict the script. A new meaningful fact (a pet, a gift, a named object) or a wrong season fails; an obvious inferred setting ("a FaceTime at eight" in a lamp-lit bedroom) is fine.
- references: Shots bind the right people and angles. The shell checks that ids exist; you check that the right person is on screen.
- staging: The blocking is believable. A shot is one action with one camera move.
- continuity: Props and the environment progress sensibly across beats. An early object returns only when the script supports it.

Not defects: one held shot per beat, a still camera, a close-up on one person, a shot under four seconds.
