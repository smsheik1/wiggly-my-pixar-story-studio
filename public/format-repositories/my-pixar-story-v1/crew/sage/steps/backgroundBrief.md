# backgroundBrief

Criteria: scene-fit, facts, spatial-action, continuity.

A background must read like a stylized animated feature set: lived-in, specific and story-dressed, with props that tell you whose space it is and when, appealing chunky stylized shapes and warm motivated light. Rooms use a muted palette with one saturated accent, and the film's signature accent stays out of the room. The rule text is in `task.craft`.

- scene-fit: The brief serves the scene's action and the script's beat.
- facts: Story props and details trace to the answers or the script. Dressing may suggest a character's age and era, but it may not assert a life fact the answers don't give: a named hobby, a pet, a gift. (A future scientist's room with science posters is right only if the answers say he loves science.)
- spatial-action: The room has the space and layout the scene's action needs.
- continuity: The room stays consistent with the master plate and with the brief's own details.
Material defects, not taste, in every background step: an empty, generic, catalog or undressed room; "plain" or "unmarked" props; "blank" or "calm" walls; a look that reads as generic 3D, photoreal or stock. "Empty of people" (no characters, people or silhouettes) is required for compositing, but an empty set is still a defect. A muted, restrained, neutral, ivory or oatmeal palette is never a failure on its own and never rescues an empty room. Quote the offending words and give a concrete story-dressing repair drawn from the answers.
